What we teach

The subjects. Taught properly.

We focus on Maths and the Sciences — the subjects where a great teacher makes the biggest difference, and where real understanding is worth far more than a memorised method.

∑

Mathematics

From number sense and problem-solving in the primary years to algebra, calculus and everything beyond. We teach the reasoning, not just the steps — so that when the question changes shape, the understanding holds.

⬡

Biology

The science of living things, taught with the curiosity it deserves. From cells and genetics to ecosystems and human biology — and the questions that science is still working to answer.

⚗

Chemistry

Atoms, reactions, energy, and the invisible architecture of everything. We make the abstract tangible — and the equations make sense when you understand what they are actually describing.

∿

Physics

Forces, waves, electricity, space — the subject that sits underneath everything else. We teach Physics as a way of seeing the world, not as a list of formulae to survive until the exam.

For UK families: we have strong experience preparing students for the 11+ and helping older students excel at GCSE. These are not separate programmes — they are the natural outcome of learning the subject well.

A short manifesto

Things we'll never do

Never Drill your child to pass a test they don't understand.
Never Sell fear. Plenty of tutoring businesses do. It works, and we won't do it.
Never Bury a nine-year-old under worksheets until the curiosity drains out of them.
Never Rank students against each other as a motivational tactic.
Never Package the life-skills sessions as an upsell. They are included. Full stop.

We'd rather grow a thinker slowly than manufacture a result quickly.

The lessons school forgets to teach

Schools teach the subjects. Almost no one teaches a child to negotiate, to disagree well, to say how they feel clearly, or to lead a room. We do — woven into every place at Kalos, never sold separately.

i

Negotiation & conflict

Monthly sessions that mix year groups for real, practised negotiations, debates and structured disagreements. Children learn to listen, hold a position, and find the deal — a skill used every week, taught nowhere on the curriculum.

ii

Communicating clearly

Saying what you mean, and naming how you feel, calmly and well — to a teacher, a sibling, a room. We practise this. Presentation, persuasion, and the quiet confidence of being genuinely understood.

iii

Initiative & ownership

Ananya's calendar presentation and the invented game are the examples. Taking an idea you care about and seeing it through. We celebrate the students who start things, not only those who finish the worksheet first.

iv

Growing as a person

Curiosity, resilience, good habits — reading widely, writing regularly, playing sport seriously. The practices that outlast any exam and quietly make the academic results easier to reach.

Included with every place at Kalos, at a low cost. We never package these as separate courses — this is simply what a Kalos education is.

Our tutors

We don't hire tutors.
We hire people worth looking up to.

Every tutor at Kalos is what we'd call an overachiever in the real sense — not someone who collected grades, but someone who has built a genuinely interesting life. They are deep experts in their subjects, and they are also athletes, readers, builders, and people with strong convictions about the world. They have done hard things outside a classroom.

Subject knowledge can be verified on a CV. Inspiration cannot. A student needs someone in the room who makes them feel — not because they said so, but because they have lived it — that being curious and working hard leads somewhere real.

When we consider a new tutor for Kalos, we ask one question above everything else: would a twelve-year-old find this person genuinely fascinating? If the answer isn't a clear yes, they are not right for us — however strong the rest of their application.

Every Kalos tutor brings the full academy philosophy into their sessions. They notice the child in front of them. They are interested in what that child reads, plays, builds, and wonders about. The holistic side of Kalos is not a separate programme — it lives in every lesson, every week.

A letter from our founder

Why I built Kalos

I'll be honest about where this started.

I kept meeting clever children who had been trained to pass and not trained to think. They could produce the method and freeze the moment a question changed shape. The spark — the thing that makes a student ask why we have twelve months and not thirteen — had been quietly, systematically pressed out of them. That bothered me deeply.

I am, by background, a competitive basketball player and an MBA from IIM Calcutta. I read across subjects constantly — history, philosophy, biology, whatever I can find. I write every day, even badly. None of that appears on a teaching qualification, and all of it, I think, is why my students learn. Not because I am impressive to them, but because I am visibly still curious in front of them. A student who sees their teacher genuinely enjoying a hard question is quietly being given permission to not yet know everything.

The conversations we have at Kalos are not always about the syllabus. We talk about books, about what it teaches you to lose badly on a basketball court and come back to training the next morning, about writing one paragraph a day until it becomes a habit, about what a student cares about outside the lesson. We take that seriously — because it matters.

I notice when something shifts. When a student who barely spoke in September is arguing a position confidently in January. When a child who dreaded Maths is explaining it to a classmate. That is the part of this work that makes me want to come back the next day.

Kalos exists because I believe the exam result is the easy part. The hard, important, permanent work is the person sitting it.

— Karan K K

Founder, Kalos Academy
