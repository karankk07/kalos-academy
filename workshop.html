The Student Workshop

This is where we share projects that arose entirely from a student's own curiosity. There were no curricula dictating these topics, no worksheet prompts, and no grades assigned. Just an interesting question, and the space to trace it down.

Presentation Ananya · Year 6

The thirteen-month calendar

What She Wondered

During an ordinary discussion about schedules, Ananya stopped and asked: "Why are there twelve months in a year — when thirteen equal ones would make so much more sense?" She challenged why we accept the complexity of uneven months (28, 30, and 31 days) when a simple, mathematically consistent alternative is staring us in the face.

What She Researched

Pursuing the question, she researched the astronomical basis of calendar structures. She investigated lunar cycles, mapping the difference between synodic and sidereal months. She tracked calendar history, discovering how political decisions in ancient Rome shaped our current system, and worked through the leap year calculations needed to align calendar days with the solar orbit.

What She Discovered

Ananya mapped out the math: $13 \text{ months} \times 28 \text{ days} = 364 \text{ days}$. This left exactly one day left over in standard years (and two in leap years). She proposed designating this extra day as a global holiday called "Year Day" that sits outside of any week or month. She was delighted to discover that her independent proposal mirrored the historical International Fixed Calendar proposed in 1902.

How She Presented It

Entirely on her own initiative, she created a slide presentation detailing her calendar design. She stood in front of the class, presented the mathematical layout, and defended its structural trade-offs—such as the fact that 13 is a prime number and cannot be divided into quarters, and the consequence that every single month contains a Friday the 13th.
